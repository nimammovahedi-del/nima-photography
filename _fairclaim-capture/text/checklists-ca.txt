00
Before you decide
8 ITEMS
CALIFORNIA BASICS
Limit
$12,500 for individuals; $6,250 for businesses. No more than two claims over $2,500 in a calendar year, anywhere in California (CCP 116.231).
Appeal
Defendant only; deadline 30 days.
Lawyers
Not allowed.
Official help
California court self-help · free small claims advisor by county

Cal. Code Civ. Proc. §§ 116.110-116.950 (esp. §§ 116.220, 116.221, 116.230, 116.231, 116.710); Gov. Code §§ 68630-68641
checked 2026-11-05
all sources on the California page

Write down what happened, what you're owed, and exactly how you got the number (receipts, invoice, deposit minus fair deductions).
Check the amount is within California's limit ($12,500 for individuals). If it's more, you may have to give up the extra or use a different court.
Check the deadline to sue for your kind of claim. In California, for example: 4 years for a written contract, 2 for an oral one, 3 for property damage (CCP 337, 339, 338).
Ask whether the other side could actually pay: do they have a job, a business, a bank account or property? A judgment is not a payment. In a Denver study, 55% of winners collected nothing.
Add up the costs: the filing fee ($30–$100 in California), serving the papers, time off work, and more fees to collect if they don't pay.
Consider other routes: a demand letter, mediation, a complaint to a licensing board or consumer agency, or deciding it isn't worth pursuing.
Know what happens if you lose, and whether either side can appeal (in California: defendant only).
Get free help if you want it: the court's self-help center, a free small claims advisor, or legal aid (LawHelp.org).
01
Ask for payment in writing
5 ITEMS
CALIFORNIA
Asking first
The claim form (SC-100) asks whether you asked the defendant to pay before suing, where that was possible. In person or by phone counts; a letter leaves a record.

CCP 116.320(b)
checked 2026-11-05

Find the other side's correct legal name and address. For a business, check the state's business-entity search.
State the amount, how you got it, and a specific deadline to pay (often 10 to 14 days).
Keep it calm and factual. No threats of criminal charges or public shaming.
Send it in a way you can prove, such as certified mail, or email plus regular mail. Keep a copy.
Write down the date you sent it and any reply. If they offer part of the amount, consider it.

02
File the claim
5 ITEMS
CALIFORNIA
Court
Small claims division of the Superior Court
Filing fee
$30–$100. $30 for claims up to $1,500; $50 for over $1,500 to $5,000; $75 for over $5,000 to $12,500; $100 if the plaintiff filed more than 12 small claims in California in the past 12 months. Same statewide. Clerk certified-mail service is $15 (offered only in some courts); sheriff and process server fees extra.
Fee waiver
FW-001 Request to Waive Court Fees (Rev. March 1, 2026); order on FW-003; info sheet FW-001-INFO. You qualify with certain public benefits, household income at or below 200% of the federal poverty guidelines (from 2026-01-01), or if paying would leave you unable to meet basic needs.
Filing online
Some courts
Hearing date
The clerk sets it 20 to 70 days after you file (CCP 116.330(a)).

Get California's small claims form from the court or its self-help site (SC-100).
Pick the right court location (venue): usually where the other side lives or does business, or where the problem happened.
Use the other side's exact legal name. Suing the wrong name can leave you with a judgment you can't collect.
Pay the filing fee, or ask for a fee waiver first if money is tight.
Keep copies of everything you file, and write down the hearing date and courtroom.

03
Serve the papers
4 ITEMS
CALIFORNIA
Allowed methods
The plaintiff cannot serve. Personal service by anyone 18+ who is not a party (friend, relative, sheriff/marshal in some counties for a fee, or registered process server); substituted service at home or work plus mailing (complete 10 days after mailing); or certified mail by the court clerk ($15, only some courts). Must be served at least 15 days before trial (20 days if served outside the county).
Deadlines
Served at least 15 days before the hearing (20 if the defendant lives outside the county). Proof of service (SC-104) filed at least 5 days before. Substituted service is complete 10 days after mailing, so start earlier.

CCP 116.340
checked 2026-11-05

Find out which ways of serving are allowed in California, and whether you may do it yourself.
Get an address where the person or business can actually be found.
Arrange service early. There is a deadline before the hearing, and late service usually means a postponement.
Make sure the proof of service is filled in and filed with the court before the hearing.

04
Go to the hearing
7 ITEMS
CALIFORNIA
Lawyers
Not allowed. Parties may consult a lawyer before court but cannot be represented by one at the small claims trial. Lawyers may represent parties after judgment and on appeal.
Postponing
Ask in writing on SC-150 at least 10 days before the hearing; $10 fee once the defendant was served on time (CCP 116.570).
If they don't come
You still have to prove your claim with evidence (CCP 116.520(b)).

CCP 116.570
CCP 116.520
checked 2026-11-05

Bring three copies of your evidence: contract, photos, messages, receipts, and a one-page timeline.
Ask witnesses to come, or find out whether the court accepts signed written statements.
Practise a two-minute summary: what was agreed, what happened, what you're owed and how you got the number.
Arrange time off. If you need an interpreter, ask the court well ahead.
If you can't make the date, ask for a postponement in writing as early as you can.
Ask whether the court offers free mediation that day.
Ask for your court costs to be added to any judgment.

05
Collect what you won
6 ITEMS
CALIFORNIA
Appeal period
Defendant only; 30 days. Collection is paused automatically for 30 days after the Notice of Entry of Judgment, or until an appeal is decided (CCP 116.810).
Statement of assets
Unless they've paid, the debtor must send you SC-133 within 30 days after notice of the judgment is mailed (CCP 116.830).
Collection fees
Debtor's exam $60; writ of execution $40 (expires after 180 days); abstract of judgment $40 plus the recorder's fee; renewal $45. Most can be added to the judgment.
Protected money
$2,325 in the debtor's bank accounts is protected automatically (July 1, 2026). Wage garnishment is capped (CCP 706.050).
How long it lasts
10 years from entry, renewable before it runs out; interest 10% a year in most cases (CCP 683.020, 685.010).

CCP 116.810
CCP 116.830

Wait until the appeal period is over, then send a polite written request for payment with a copy of the judgment.
If they don't pay, find out what they have: a debtor's examination makes them answer questions under oath.
Learn the collection tools and their fees: writ of execution, wage garnishment, bank levy, and a lien on property.
Remember that some money and property is protected (exemptions). Someone who is judgment-proof may not be able to pay now.
Keep a record of every payment. Once you're paid in full, file a satisfaction of judgment.
Note when the judgment expires, and renew it in time if it's still unpaid.
