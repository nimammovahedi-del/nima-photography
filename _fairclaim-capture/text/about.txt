GETTING PAID

Winning isn't getting paid.

Courts don't track whether winners collect. The few studies that looked found that many never do.

One study of Denver's small claims court

55%

of winners never collected anything

of plaintiffs who came to the hearing won

of all the money awarded was actually paid

WHAT A JUDGMENT DOES
It lets the winner use the court's collection tools, such as a wage garnishment or a bank levy. It doesn't make the other side pay. Step 5: collecting.

WHY THIS SITE EXISTS

Information about small claims court that isn't trying to talk you into anything.

The rules, the costs and the odds of being paid, side by side. You decide.

A
Free

No ads, no sponsors, nothing for sale, no accounts. It isn't paid for by anyone.

B
Private

No analytics, no tracking cookies, no third-party scripts or fonts. What you type stays in your tab.

C
Sourced and dated

Every rule links to the law or the court, with the date we last checked it.

D
Neutral

It won't talk you into suing, or out of it. Costs and the odds of being paid sit next to the steps.

WHO IT'S FOR
Tenants
A landlord kept a security deposit.
Customers
A business or contractor owes a refund or a repair.
Small businesses
An invoice went unpaid. Some states set a lower limit for businesses.
People who were sued
A guide for defendants is planned. State pages link to each court's guide.

INFORMATION, NOT ADVICE

Fair Claim explains the rules. Only someone who knows your facts can tell you what to do.

WHAT YOU GET HERE

Legal information

How the rules and the process work in general: the limit, which form starts a case, how long you have to appeal.

WHAT YOU WON'T

Legal advice

Whether your claim is good, how much to ask for, what to say. Our tools do arithmetic and fill in templates; they don't judge your case.
